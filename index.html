Post-Doctoral Researcher & Lecturer

Chair of Political Sociology & Social Policy, HU Berlin

In April 2025, I joined the Department of Social Sciences at Humboldt-University of Berlin, where I currently work as a post-doctoral researcher and lecturer, affiliated with Prof. Hanna Schwander's Chair of Political Sociology and Social Policy. Since July 2025, I additionally serve as Acting Academic Manager of the Berlin Graduate School of Sciences.

Previously, I completed my PhD in Political Science at the University of Mannheim, graduating magna cum laude under the supervision of Prof. Richard Traunmüller, and hold an M.A. in Political Science (LMU Munich) and a B.A. in International Journalism (University of Applied Sciences Bremen).

In my dissertation, I examine the democratic consequences of climate change-related attitudes and concerns — showing that rising climate concern is causally related to reduced satisfaction with democracy, and using an original double list experiment to uncover citizens' willingness to support undemocratic measures in pursuit of their climate political agenda.

Beyond climate change, my research interests span political participation and trust, social equality, and democratic resilience — including candidate gender cues in elections, the political inclusion of citizens with low literacy, and attitudes towards militant democracy. Methodologically, I rely primarily on surveys and survey experiments.

Prior to Berlin, I was a doctoral research assistant at the Research Institute Social Cohesion at Goethe-University Frankfurt, and worked as a lecturer at the University of Mannheim.

The Underlying Structure of Political Support

Menzner, J. and D. Schweizer

Politics & Governance, 14

Abstract

Using 29 waves of the German Longitudinal Election Study, we test the dimensionality of political support. Specific support (e.g. satisfaction with government) fluctuates substantially, while diffuse support (e.g. institutional trust) is more stable. Both load onto a single latent trait, and changes in either indicator tend to predict subsequent changes in the other — evidence of self-reinforcing dynamics rather than a buffering "reservoir of goodwill."

The Importance of Descriptive Representation & Cue-Taking in Free List Local Elections

Menzner, J., C. Schmid and L. Rettig

German Politics

Abstract

Analyzing the 2024 Mannheim municipal elections — where voters distributed 48 votes among 508 candidates on a free-list ballot — we find party affiliation, ballot position, and incumbency significantly shape vote totals, while gender and occupation cues show no direct effect. Candidates gain markedly from running in their own residential district and from an age profile matching that district's demographics.

Securing Moral Authority: A Cross-National Analysis of Protestant Engagement in the Implementation of Morality Policies in Western Europe

Mettang, O., E.-M. Euchner, L. Fischer and J. Menzner

VOLUNTAS

Abstract

Examining Protestant churches' involvement in four morality policy areas across Germany, England, and Denmark, we show that variation in religious engagement is driven mainly by churches' room to maneuver and policy congruence with state goals — governance capacity plays a secondary role. Providing social services remains a means through which churches can still exert moral authority.

The Potential of Open Lists for the Election of Women to the German Bundestag. Results of a Survey Experiment

Rudolph, L., Däubler, T. and J. Menzner

Politische Vierteljahresschrift  ·  1st Prize, Fritz-Thyssen-Foundation Award

Abstract

In a quota-representative survey experiment (N=2,640), we test whether German voters would choose female candidates from open party lists. Both female and male voters factor in candidate gender as expected, female candidates are rarely disadvantaged overall, and voters tend to level out gender-imbalanced lists — evidence that open lists merit more attention in electoral reform debates.

Subjective Freedom of Speech: Why Do Citizens Think They Cannot Speak Freely?

Menzner, J. and R. Traunmüller

Politische Vierteljahresschrift

Abstract

The first systematic study of subjective free speech in Germany, using a pre-registered analysis of GLES 2021 data. Three factors are significantly and consistently related to citizens feeling they cannot speak freely: political preferences, populist attitudes, and identification with the Alternative for Germany party.

Citizens' Two-Sided Demand for Undemocratic Climate Governance

Abstract

Using original German survey data (N=2,101) and two list experiments, I show that stronger climate preferences — for or against action — are robustly linked to support for non-democratic governance, including restricting activism, ignoring pro-climate court rulings, and censoring denial. 'True' support for undemocratic climate (in)action reaches roughly 35–39%, with skeptics least willing to disclose it openly.

National, Partisan or Ideological Biases? How EU Officials' Characteristics can Influence Infringement Proceeding Outcomes

with Johannes Lattmann

Abstract

Combining 19,352 EU infringement proceedings (2002–2020) with data on responsible officials, we find a nationality bias among Commissioners overseeing cases from their home country — but no evidence of bias linked to ideology or partisanship.

A Case of Strategic Issue Emphasis: Comparing Easy-Read and Regular Manifestos Using NLP

with Lukas Isermann & Julius Diener · presented MZES 2024, CompText 2023

Abstract

NLP analysis of 62 easy-read manifestos from five German parties shows welfare issues over-emphasised by 10 percentage points while economic issues are downplayed — parties appear to use easy-read manifestos for strategic gain rather than equal political participation.

How Growing Climate Concern Erodes Political Support

with David Schweizer · presented DVPW AK Umwelt 2023, EPSA 2023

Abstract

Applying random-effects-within-between models to German panel data (2010–2021) across nine concerns, we show climate concern is causally linked to declining democratic satisfaction — an effect that has grown stronger in recent waves and is amplified among Green partisans and post-materialists.

Uncovering Uneven Class Awareness: A Heteroskedastic Regression Approach to Subjective Class Placement

with Franziska Veit

Abstract

Objective class markers (education, income, occupational prestige) predict subjective class more precisely for those who score highly on them; citizens from objectively lower classes show more dispersed subjective perceptions.

Losing Satisfaction or Withdrawing Support? How Climate Change Concerns Shape Democratic Support

Abstract

Using original German panel data (2021–2023, N>10,000) and pre-registered mediation analyses, I trace whether climate concern moves citizens from specific evaluations (government trust) to diffuse ones (support for democracy itself) — a chain moderated by climate salience and attenuated by political efficacy.
